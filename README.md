## Thesis Overview

This thesis explores the design of a predictive mood-tracking mobile application for people living with bipolar disorder.

The project investigates how artificial intelligence, user-reported data, and user-centred design can support mood monitoring, medication adherence, early awareness of possible mood changes, and collaboration with healthcare professionals and trusted support persons.

The proposed application was designed as a conceptual prototype in Figma, with particular attention to usability, accessibility, emotional support, privacy, and clear communication.

## Research Objectives

The main objectives of the thesis are to:

- analyse the limitations of existing bipolar disorder tracking applications;
- investigate the potential role of AI in mood prediction;
- identify user needs related to mood, medication, sleep, and daily activity tracking;
- design an accessible and emotionally supportive mobile experience;
- explore collaborative care features for patients, doctors, and trusted support persons;
- consider privacy, consent, transparency, and ethical use of sensitive health data.

## Design Approach

The project follows a user-centred design approach informed by:

- literature review;
- analysis of existing mood-tracking applications;
- clinical and digital-health research;
- requirement definition;
- wireframing and interface prototyping;
- accessibility and emotional-design principles;
- ethical and privacy considerations.

## Main Features Explored

- daily mood entry and symptom tracking;
- medication and routine reminders;
- sleep and activity monitoring;
- visualisation of mood trends;
- AI-supported mood forecasting;
- personalised motivational messages and activity suggestions;
- role-based dashboards for patients, doctors, and trusted support persons;
- permission-based data sharing;
- privacy and consent controls.

## Scope and Limitations

This thesis presents a conceptual design and research framework. It does not include:

- clinical validation;
- patient trials;
- a production-ready application;
- a deployed AI model;
- real-world diagnostic use;
- long-term outcome evaluation.

The proposed system would require future validation with patients, clinicians, caregivers, and technical specialists before any real-world healthcare use.
